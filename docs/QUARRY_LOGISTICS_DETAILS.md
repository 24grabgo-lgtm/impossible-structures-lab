# Quarry Logistics Details

**Focus:** Step-by-step of getting a megablock *out of the rock and onto the road* — with Baalbek as the extreme case and general megalith practice as backup.

---

## 1. Why logistics starts *before* the first pull

At Baalbek-scale mass, **you do not “find a stone and drag it.”** You:
1. Choose a bed that already wants to be a block (bedding planes)
2. Shape it so transport mass is minimum
3. Build the **path and control machines** before the final free
4. Free it onto rollers/cradle without a lethal tip
5. Haul under continuous brake/steer authority

Abandoned quarry giants (Hajar el-Hibla, larger 2014 block) are frozen mid-logistics — the best tutorial levels antiquity left.

---

## 2. Extraction sequence (Baalbek-oriented)

### 2.1 Layout on the bed
Nummulitic limestone beds can **set the height** of the megalith: top and bottom often follow natural stratification. Crews cut **vertical trenches** to isolate the plan shape rather than inventing thickness from solid mass every time.

### 2.2 Trenches
- Smaller blocks: trenches often **~10–20 cm** wide  
- Giant faces: trenches reported **~40–60 cm** so workers can stand in the cut and work deeper  
- Three sides freed first is common on unfinished monoliths; the fourth / base is the dangerous release

### 2.3 Reference lines
Carved guide lines on faces (seen on Hibla-type stones) keep multiple quarrymen cutting to the same plane — parallel labor, one geometry.

### 2.4 Base trench + wedges
A narrow basal cut; wedges driven until a continuous crack runs. Crack evidence under unfinished blocks is treated as the release signature.

### 2.5 Undercut synchronized with support
As the base is dug (often described **east→west following slope** at Hibla), **logs/rollers are fed under** the stone so it never drops onto dead rock. Ropes in **chiselled holes** on stone and quarry faces hold the mass while the last bridges are killed.

### 2.6 Lewis / peg / rope holes
Square and round holes on blocks and quarry floor = **anchor points** for holding ropes, elevating gear, or tying to capstan systems — logistics hardware carved into the workflow, not decoration.

**Failure modes for the game:** premature free → tip/crush; uneven undercut → twist crack; no anchors → runaway on slope.

## 3. Transfer to transport envelope

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Dress in quarry | Reduce volume before the road (Adam: move minimum mass) |
| Cradle | Rollers and/or heavy timber sledge |
| Load skill | Getting *onto* the sledge is its own hazard (Egyptian experimental work stresses this) |
| Soft ground kill | Sand/soft fill stops movement — need **prepared way** |

Rollers alone on soft or irregular ground are widely criticized in modern experimental literature; **fixed track + sledge** is the more controllable pattern for serious mass.

---

## 4. The way (track logistics)

### 4.1 What “prepared” means
- Timber balks / beam carriageway  
- Rammed earth / clay / gypsum-class hard surfaces (Egyptian parallel)  
- Drainage so the way does not turn to soup  
- Optional lubrication: water, wet clay, fat  

### 4.2 Adam’s friction claim (model knob)
Classic reconstruction: a firm beam road can cut effective haul demand toward **~1/12 of dead weight** (e.g. 800 t → ~66.7 t-force class equivalent in his arithmetic). **Use as a sim multiplier for “path quality,” not gospel.**

### 4.3 Grade strategy
Two competing story emphases in the literature:
- **Quarry slightly above sanctuary** → braking/steering on descent  
- **Raise successive carriageways** with each podium course so “vertical lift” is absorbed into an always-level haul to the current bed  

Both are excellent **player choices**: build up the road vs fight the grade.

### 4.4 Distance
Baalbek: **~800–900 m to 1 km** class — short, but every metre is under kiloton stress.

---

## 5. Motive power & control (capstan logistics)

### 5.1 Why capstans show up
Direct rope pull at 800 t is chaos. **Capstans + pulleys** give slow, repeatable force and holding power. Site archaeology: **circular wear / rope friction traces** near Hibla interpreted as capstan stations; anchor rope marks on quarry floor.

### 5.2 Adam-scale crew math (order-of-magnitude)
| Setup | Rough figures in the literature |
|-------|----------------------------------|
| Smaller reconstruction | ~6 machines, ~144 men, tens of tonnes traction after losses |
| Heavy trilithon reconstruction | **16 capstans × ~32 men ≈ 512 men**; pulley trains; friction eats large share; total draught cited near **~2/3 of load** in one formulation |

Oxen appear in thought experiments but are often dismissed for **space and control** on a tight quarry–temple corridor.

### 5.3 Control roles (staff the haul)
- Haul teams / capstan crews  
- **Brake crews** uphill of a descending load  
- Roller/sleeper recycling parties (Assyrian relief logic: bring rollers to the front)  
- Spotters for twist, crack, rope stretch  
- Way repair gang  

**Any mistake → total loss of the block and likely fatalities** — logistics as risk management.

---

## 6. Throughput (how many stones)

Podium accounting in secondary literature: on the order of **~30 × ~400 t** class blocks plus **3 × ~800 t** trilithon stones from the quarry system — i.e. this was a **repeatable industrial process**, not one miracle move.

## 7. General megalith logistics (not only Baalbek)

| Pattern | Where it shows up |
|---------|-------------------|
| Wedge free + sledge off platform | Bluestone quarry models (Wales) |
| Sledge on prepared track; rollers problematic | Experimental critique vs popular roller imagery |
| Water on track as lubricant | Egyptian reliefs / experiments |
| Ice path + wells (seasonal) | Later Chinese long-haul parallels |
| Intermediate monument near quarry | Proposed for some bluestone narratives |

Baalbek is the **mass extreme on a short leash**. Stonehenge is **moderate mass on a long leash**. Your lab can teach both.

---

## 8. Player systems map

| System | Actions |
|--------|---------|
| **Survey** | Bed height, defects, trench plan |
| **Cut** | Vertical trenches, guide lines, basal crack |
| **Support** | Feed rollers under; tension anchors |
| **Free** | Kill bridges; listen for crack; emergency hold |
| **Way** | Build path quality, grade, lube, repair |
| **Station** | Place capstans, anchors, brake points |
| **Haul** | Phase pulls; recycle supports; never lose brake |
| **Abort** | Hibla ending — leave the giant in the cut |

---

## 9. Design law

**Logistics is the game between “cut” and “placed.”**  
If the player only experiences a loading screen between quarry and temple, Baalbek has been wasted.

---

*Trenches, anchors, way, capstans, brakes — then, and only then, the stone moves.*
