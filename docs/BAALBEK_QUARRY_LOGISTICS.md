# Baalbek Quarry Logistics

**Status:** Research reference for stretch scenario  
**Stance:** Engineering parameters + competing hypotheses — not a verdict.

---

## 1. The stones

| Stone | Approx size / mass | Status |
|-------|--------------------|--------|
| **Trilithon** (3 blocks in Jupiter podium) | ~19.1–19.6 m long × ~4.2 m high × ~3.6 m deep; often cited **~750–800 t** each | In place in podium wall |
| **Hajar el-Hibla** (Stone of the Pregnant Woman) | Often ~**1000 t** class; still in quarry | Never fully moved; partially freed |
| **Larger quarry megalith** (reported 2014 area work) | Claims up to ~**1650 t** class | Still in quarry; extraction incomplete |

Underlying podium courses include many **~300–400 t** class blocks as well.

---

## 2. Distance & topography (the logistics core)

- Quarry (Sheikh Abdallah / South quarry area) is **close**: commonly cited **~800–900 m** from the sanctuary — not a multi-day inter-regional haul like Stonehenge bluestones.
- **Elevation relationship is contested in popular summaries:**
  - Some accounts stress quarry **slightly higher** → mostly **controlled descent / braking** problem.
  - Other engineering narratives stress **raising onto the podium** and even claim **net uphill** components to final bed height (~tens of metres of structural height matter even if the haul road is short).
- **Design implication for the lab:** treat **horizontal distance as short** and **vertical/placement as the brutal part** — haul is hard; **seating on the wall line** is harder.

---

## 3. Quarry practice (what the site suggests)

- Blocks **dressed in the quarry** to move minimum mass/volume (Adam and later site work).
- Trenches, undercutting, wedges; monoliths left on temporary rock bridges/shelves until final free.
- **Lewis holes / rope anchor marks** and **capstan wear traces** reported around Hajar el-Hibla area excavations — evidence for **organized rope/capstan control**, not only pure muscle drag.
- Abandoned giants are the best “paused logistics” screenshots antiquity left: cut, not delivered.

## 4. Mainstream-leaning transport models

| Element | Proposal |
|---------|----------|
| **Track** | Prepared timber balks / firm carriageway; lubrication (water, clay, fat) |
| **Friction cut** | Adam-style claim: good rolling path can cut effective haul demand dramatically (order-of **1/12** of dead weight cited in classic reconstruction — treat as **model parameter**, verify in sim) |
| **Motive power** | Large human teams; oxen discussions exist but coordination/whip problems at this mass; **capstans + pulley blocks** repeatedly invoked |
| **Capstan scale (Adam line)** | Example reconstruction: **16 capstans × ~32 men ≈ 512** generating large draught after pulley advantage (friction eats a large share) |
| **Path** | Short prepared way from quarry to podium; earth banks / rollers in stages |
| **Placement** | Earthen ramps against rising wall and/or incremental lever + crib — **least evidenced, most argued** |

Historical analogues often cited in this literature: **Luxor** obelisk moves, **Carrara**, and the **Russian Thunder Stone** (~1500 t class granite move for the Bronze Horseman pedestal) as proof that **early modern** crews could move kiloton-class stone with wood, iron, and organization — not that Romans used the same recipe.

---

## 5. Open / alternative mechanical ideas

- **Rolling flange hypothesis** (recent experimental-archaeology adjacent proposal): sacrificial side flanges cut on the block so it *rolls* on prepared ways, then flanges dressed off — aims to fix crushing/friction limits of plain rollers under 800 t.
- Pre-Roman podium theories: separate **who ordered the stones** from **how any crew moves 800 t**; the lab should not require a chronological choice to run the physics.
- Claims that “no reconstruction fully works” appear in popular engineering media; academic Roman-engineering papers argue **extreme but continuous** with known tech. **Both attitudes are flags the player can stress-test.**

---

## 6. Logistics chain (player-facing)

1. **Select face** — jointing, defect survey  
2. **Channel + undercut** — leave rock bridges  
3. **Dress to transport envelope**  
4. **Build way** — timber road / banks / drainage  
5. **Free + transfer to cradle** — highest fracture/accident risk  
6. **Haul with brake teams** — distance short; control is everything  
7. **Raise to bed height** — ramp and/or crib/lever  
8. **Final dress / joint** — optional precision mode
